(a) Remove the damaged plies from laminates and sandwich structure areas using No. 80
sandpaper, fine abrasive, or trim out the damaged surface, keeping a geometric shape
(oval, circular or rectangular), exercising care not to damage the honeycomb or the sur-
face around the damaged area.

(b) Mark the area to receive the repair layers with adhesive tape, leaving 1-in (25-mm) mar-

gin for the additional layer to settle.

(c) Lightly sand the surface within the marked area, so as to achieve a good adhesion.

(d) Remove the adhesive tape and clean surface with MEK (Methil Ethil Ketone), at least

1/2 inches (13 mm) beyond the limits of the sanded area.
NOTE: Allow the solvent to evaporate for a few minutes before proceeding with the
repair.
D. Replacement of plies.

(1) Use glass fabric preimpregnated with epoxy resin style 7781.

NOTE: Use an additional repair ply (the same fabric as that originally used in the top ply in
the case of hybrid composites) so as to minimize the surface depression when plies
are replaced.

(2) Cut replacement plies to required dimensions. There should be a 1 in (25 mm) overlap.

(3) Manually impregnate fabrics using epoxy resin (Table 2 of SRM chapter 51-70-01, lamination
epoxy resin A, B, C, or D).

(4) Laminate first ply in the position in which it will be saturated with resin. Repeat the procedure
for every ply. The repair plies must be installed in the same sequence as that of the original
plies being repaired, in the case of hybrid composites.

E. Cure of Overlapping Plies (figure 604).

(1) Place a thermocouple in the interface between repair area and remaining structure.

(2) Place perforated parting film and bleeder cloth layers, interspersed in the repair as follows:
install one layer of perforated parking film and one layer of bleeder cloth for two plies of
repair fabric.

(3) Install a vacuum line over the top bleeder cloth outside the repair area.

(4) Apply extruded sealing compound all around the area.

(5) Cover the whole repair area with a vacuum film, thus forming a vacuum bag.

EFFECTIVITY: ALL
